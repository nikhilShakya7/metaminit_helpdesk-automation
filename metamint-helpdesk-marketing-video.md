# Metamint Helpdesk Marketing Video

## 1. Product analysis

The materials identify the product as **Metamint Helpdesk**, a WordPress support plugin with a customer-facing chat widget and support portal, plus an agent dashboard. The strongest verified story is the handoff between those experiences: customers can ask questions or submit and follow up on tickets, while agents can reply and manage ticket details in WordPress.

The feature evidence comes from the project's [README](./README.md), [test documentation](./docs/tests.md), and screenshots of the [chat widget](./docs/screenshots/widget-conversations.png), [FAQ search](./docs/screenshots/widget-faq.png), [customer ticket list](./docs/screenshots/portal-my-tickets.png), [ticket form](./docs/screenshots/portal-create-ticket.png), and [agent ticket view](./docs/screenshots/admin-ticket-view.png).

- **Likely audience:** WordPress site owners and customer-support teams who want a structured way to handle customer conversations and tickets.
- **Customer need:** A clear place to ask for help, look for answers, submit an issue, and return to an existing ticket.
- **Agent need:** A dashboard for reviewing conversations, replying, and managing ticket status, priority, assignee, and department.
- **Best features to demonstrate:** The embedded support widget; FAQ and notices; ticket submission and follow-up in the customer portal; agent reply and ticket controls in the WordPress dashboard.
- **Claims to avoid:** Response-time or productivity statistics, security guarantees, or claims about automation or integrations not established by the materials. The project documentation also notes issues with admin status and priority filters, so the video should not present those filters as working results.
- **Production note:** The supplied materials use the local address `metamint.local`, not a public product URL. Record on an approved public or staging instance before release. The screenshots also show Metamint branding alongside a RoboDesk widget mark; confirm the approved product naming and logo before publishing.

## 2. Marketing concept and creative direction

**Concept:** *A clearer path from customer question to support response.*

Open on the familiar friction of a customer unsure where to turn. Then show the actual product guiding them from the website widget to self-service answers or a ticket, before revealing the agent's reply and ticket controls. Keep the product UI authentic; use any AI-generated footage only for brief, generic human-context shots.

**Look and feel:** Polished, reassuring SaaS advertising. Use the interface's white space, green portal accents, blue widget header, and the approved product logo. Use restrained motion and typography so the real interface remains the focus.

## 5. Screen-recording instructions

1. **Prepare a safe demo environment.** Use an approved public or staging instance and demo accounts. Replace the local test data with a clean, realistic support scenario. Do not show credentials, private customer information, browser autofill, test names, or internal URLs.
2. **Capture the widget.** Open the actual site, expand the support widget, and record its conversation and FAQ views. Search for a real FAQ entry. Include notices only if there is approved content to show.
3. **Capture the customer journey.** Navigate to the actual portal's Create Ticket page, submit the demo request, then open My Tickets and the new ticket's reply view. Use cuts where needed to fit the runtime.
4. **Capture the agent journey.** Open the corresponding ticket in the WordPress dashboard. Record an agent reply and the real status, priority, assignee, and department controls. Avoid using bulk actions or status/priority filters as the feature proof.
5. **Keep the UI honest.** Record the real product at native resolution. Add crops, cursor emphasis, and callouts in editing; don't replace or redraw product controls. Verify all visible interface text and demo content before export.

## 6. AI video-generation prompts for supporting visuals

Use generated footage only for the opening customer-context shot. **Do not use AI to generate, alter, or imitate the product interface, logo, or readable product text.**

**Opening shot prompt:**

> Premium SaaS commercial, realistic small-business customer at a tidy desk using a laptop, momentarily unsure where to request help, subtle pause and thoughtful expression, soft natural daylight, clean neutral workspace, cinematic but understated, slow camera push-in, shallow depth of field, no visible brand names, no legible text, no readable user interface, no logos, widescreen 16:9, professional and empathetic tone.

If the generated shot includes any readable screen content or invented branding, crop or replace it with a real product recording.

## 7. Editing, music, and subtitle recommendations

- **Music:** Modern, understated instrumental bed with a light pulse and warm tonal layers; no vocals. Keep it below narration and build gently toward the agent-view scene.
- **Transitions:** Prefer clean cuts between product pages, subtle dissolves for the opening-to-product transition, and restrained motion graphics. Avoid flashy transitions that compete with the interface.
- **Motion:** Use slow crops and brief highlights around real controls. Don't animate values, ticket counts, or results that weren't captured in the actual product.
- **Typography:** Use a clean, high-legibility sans serif. Keep captions concise and visually consistent with the approved branding.
- **Captions:** Burn in accurate, sentence-cased subtitles; keep them within social-safe margins. Provide a separate caption file for platforms that support it.
- **Exports:** Create a 16:9 master for the website, YouTube, and LinkedIn; reframe carefully for vertical social formats rather than shrinking the UI until it is unreadable.
